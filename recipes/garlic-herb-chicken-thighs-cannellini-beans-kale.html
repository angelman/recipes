Garlic-Herb Chicken Thighs with Cannellini Beans & Kale Cooked ✓

Servings: 5 lunch portions

Per serving: ~460 cal, ~40g protein, ~9g fiber, ~18g fat, ~28g carbs per serving

From: Oct 5, 2026 (full write-up; picked Oct 4)

meal-prepchickenone-pot

Braised chicken thighs with beans and kale. Cooked as the Oct 10 batch.

Ingredients

  • 1.75 lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 bunches lacinato kale, stems removed, leaves chopped
  • 1 jar (8 oz) sun-dried tomatoes, drained and sliced
  • 2 cups low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 sprig fresh rosemary (leaves stripped and minced), plus extra for garnish
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp salt, ½ tsp black pepper, ¼ tsp red pepper flakes
  • Juice of ½ lemon

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken thighs dry and season both sides with salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Sear the thighs 4–5 minutes per side until browned. Transfer to a plate (they don't need to be cooked through).
  3. Reduce heat to medium. Add garlic, sun-dried tomatoes, and rosemary; sauté 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the chicken broth and scrape up the browned bits. Add the beans and nestle the chicken back in.
  5. Simmer uncovered 15–20 minutes, until the chicken registers 165°F and the broth thickens slightly.
  6. Stir in the kale in batches, cover, and cook 4–5 minutes until just wilted — it should keep some body.
  7. Shred or slice the chicken into bite-sized pieces, stir back through, and finish with lemon juice. Taste and adjust salt.
  8. Divide into 5 containers. Keeps 4 days refrigerated; freezes up to 2 months.

Notes

  • Tip: if you're feeding two on Tuesday, this scales cleanly — just double everything and use a full bunch of kale per person.
  • Rice-serving tip: cook the rice separately — don't add it to the braise, or it'll go mushy sitting in the liquid all week. Just portion rice + braise into the same containers. Go with ½ cup cooked per serving (~110 calories). The beans already bring plenty of carbs and fiber, so a modest scoop keeps the lunch from becoming a food coma. That puts each portion around ~570 calories, ~42g protein, ~11g fiber.
