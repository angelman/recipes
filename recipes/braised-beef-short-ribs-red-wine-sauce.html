Braised Beef Short Ribs in Red Wine Sauce

Servings: 5

Difficulty: Medium

Per serving: ~545 cal, ~44g protein per serving (assumes all sauce consumed)

From: Oct 17, 2026

By Nagi — RecipeTin Eats

Source: recipetineats.com

beefbraisedslow-cook

Fall-apart beef short ribs braised in a red wine sauce. Simple ingredients, mostly hands-off — oven is best, but stove, slow cooker, and pressure cooker all work.

Ingredients

  • 5–6 beef short ribs, 300–400 g / 10–14 oz each
  • 1½ tsp each salt and pepper
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 large onion, chopped (brown, yellow, or white)
  • 2 celery ribs, chopped
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 cups (500 ml) dry red wine
  • 2 cups (500 ml) beef stock/broth, low sodium
  • 2 sprigs thyme (optional)
  • 2 bay leaves

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 160°C / 325°F. Season the ribs all over with the salt and pepper.
  2. Brown the ribs aggressively on all sides in the oil in a large ovenproof pot, working in batches. Remove and set aside.
  3. In the same pot over medium heat, cook the onion and garlic for 2 minutes, then add the carrot and celery and cook 5 minutes until the carrot softens. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the wine and stock, add the thyme and bay leaves, and stir until the tomato paste dissolves.
  5. Return the ribs to the pot, arranging them submerged as best you can. Cover and braise in the oven for 3 hours, until the meat pulls apart easily with forks.
  6. Lift the ribs out carefully and keep warm. Strain the sauce (optional, for a smooth sauce), return it to the pot, and simmer to reduce and thicken; season with salt and pepper. Spoon over the ribs and serve.

Notes

  • Other cook methods: stove — 2.5 hours on a low simmer, lid on; slow cooker — 8 hours on low or 5 hours on high (sear and sauté first, then reduce the strained sauce in a skillet); pressure cooker — 1 hour on high, natural release 10 minutes.
  • Non-alcoholic substitute for the 2 cups wine: non-alcoholic wine, or ½ cup extra low-sodium beef broth + 1½ cups water + 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce.
  • Wine: a good-value full-bodied red (cabernet sauvignon, merlot, or shiraz). No need for an expensive bottle; pinot is too light.
  • The sauce reduces by about half and should reach a syrupy consistency. If it's too fatty, let it sit in a jug so the fat rises, then skim it off.
  • Also works with other slow-cooking cuts (chuck cut into large pieces, shanks, osso buco, brisket) — adjust time to the size of the meat.
